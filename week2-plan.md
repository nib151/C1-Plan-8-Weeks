# 📚 C1 English Certificate — Weekly Study Plan
## Week 2: Building Confidence

---

### 🎯 Weekly Theme & Learning Objectives

This week is all about **stepping boldly into C1 territory**. You'll begin working with complex grammatical structures that signal sophistication and precision in your language use, while also stretching your listening stamina with longer, more demanding audio content. By Friday, you'll have drafted your first opinion essay — a cornerstone of C1 writing — and you'll start to feel the difference between B2 and genuine C1 expression.

---

## 📅 Day-by-Day Breakdown

---

### 🗓️ Monday — *Grammar Meets Expression*

**Daily Focus:** Complex grammar structures + fluent spoken opinion

---

#### 🗣️ Speaking — *Expressing Opinions with Sophistication*
- **Activity:** Choose a thought-provoking question (e.g. *"Should social media platforms be regulated by governments?"*). Record yourself speaking for **2–3 minutes** without stopping. On your first attempt, speak naturally. On your second attempt, consciously incorporate **inversion for emphasis** (e.g. *"Not only does this raise ethical concerns, but it also..."*) and **cleft sentences** (e.g. *"It is the lack of accountability that makes this so dangerous."*)
- **Resource:** [BBC Learning English – Grammar in Chunks (Inversion)](https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/english/course/upper-intermediate/unit-1/session-4)
- **⏱️ Time:** 30 minutes

---

#### 👂 Listening — *Extended News Listening*
- **Activity:** Watch a **BBC News Analysis** segment or a **BBC In Our Time** podcast episode (choose a topic you find genuinely interesting — history, science, culture). Listen **once without pausing** and jot down the main argument and three supporting points. Then listen again, pausing to catch specific vocabulary and phrases you missed.
- **Resource:** [BBC In Our Time Podcast](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b006qykl/episodes/downloads) | [BBC World Service](https://www.bbc.co.uk/worldservice)
- **⏱️ Time:** 35 minutes

---

#### 🔄 Mediation — *Summarising a News Article*
- **Activity:** Read a short article from **The Guardian** on any current affairs topic. Write a **neutral, concise summary** (80–100 words) as if you were explaining the key points to a friend who hasn't read it. Focus on **reformulating** ideas in your own words — avoid copying phrases directly. This is the foundation of mediation at C1.
- **Resource:** [The Guardian – World News](https://www.theguardian.com/world)
- **⏱️ Time:** 25 minutes

---

#### ✍️ Writing — *Grammar Accuracy Drill*
- **Activity:** Complete a **targeted grammar exercise** focusing on **mixed conditionals** and **passive constructions with reporting verbs** (e.g. *"It is widely believed that..."*, *"The policy is said to have caused..."*). Write **5 original sentences** using each structure in a context related to society or technology — topics common in C1 writing tasks.
- **Resource:** [British Council – Grammar Reference C1](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/grammar/c1-grammar) | [Perfect English Grammar](https://www.perfect-english-grammar.com)
- **⏱️ Time:** 30 minutes

---

#### 📖 Reading — *Identifying Authorial Stance*
- **Activity:** Read a **comment or opinion piece** from The Guardian or The Economist. As you read, annotate (on paper or digitally) where the author expresses **opinion, concession, and emphasis**. Highlight language that signals stance (e.g. *"Arguably...", "While it may be true that..., the reality is..."*). This skill is directly tested in C1 reading comprehension.
- **Resource:** [The Economist – Open Access Articles](https://www.economist.com) | [The Guardian – Opinion](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree)
- **⏱️ Time:** 30 minutes

---

### 🗓️ Tuesday — *Listening Stamina & Formal Register*

**Daily Focus:** Extended listening + formal writing register

---

#### 🗣️ Speaking — *Structured Monologue Practice*
- **Activity:** Choose a **TED Talk topic** (see resource below). Before watching, read only the title and predict what arguments the speaker might make. Then practise giving your **own 2-minute spoken response** to the topic. Focus on using **discourse markers** to organise your speech: *"To begin with...", "Furthermore...", "It could be argued that...", "On balance..."*
- **Resource:** [TED Talks – Ideas Worth Spreading](https://www.ted.com/talks) *(Recommended: any talk under 15 minutes on education, technology, or society)*
- **⏱️ Time:** 30 minutes

---

#### 👂 Listening — *TED Talk Deep Listening*
- **Activity:** Watch the **same TED Talk** you chose for speaking. First viewing: no subtitles, focus on gist and structure. Second viewing: use English subtitles only to verify comprehension. Write down **5 sophisticated phrases or collocations** the speaker uses that you could incorporate into your own speaking or writing.
- **Resource:** [TED Talks](https://www.ted.com/talks)
- **⏱️ Time:** 40 minutes

---

#### 🔄 Mediation — *Cross-Language Concept Transfer*
- **Activity:** Find a short article or social media post **in your native language** about a current issue. Write a **100-word English summary** of it, as if you were briefing an English-speaking colleague. Pay attention to **cultural concepts that don't translate directly** — how do you explain them clearly in English? This is a core C1 mediation competency.
- **Resource:** Any reputable news source in your native language
- **⏱️ Time:** 25 minutes

---

#### ✍️ Writing — *Formal Register Awareness*
- **Activity:** Take **5 informal sentences** and rewrite them in a formal register appropriate for a C1 essay or report. Example: *"Lots of people think social media is bad for kids"* → *"A significant proportion of the population holds the view that social media has a detrimental effect on young people."* Then write a **formal introduction paragraph** (60–80 words) for the essay question: *"To what extent has technology improved the quality of modern life?"*
- **Resource:** [Cambridge English – Writing at C1](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/learning-english/activities-for-learners/?skill=writing)
- **⏱️ Time:** 35 minutes

---

#### 📖 Reading — *Vocabulary in Context*
- **Activity:** Read an article from **BBC Future or BBC Culture** and identify **10 C1-level words or phrases** you don't know or wouldn't naturally use. For each one: write the word, its meaning in context, its part of speech, and one original example sentence. Don't just look words up — **notice how they function** in the sentence around them.
- **Resource:** [BBC Future](https://www.bbc.com/future) | [BBC Culture](https://www.bbc.com/culture)
- **⏱️ Time:** 30 minutes

### 🗓️ Wednesday — *Writing Workshop Day*

**Daily Focus:** Opinion essay drafting — your first full C1 writing task

---

#### 🗣️ Speaking — *Brainstorming Aloud*
- **Activity:** Before writing your essay, **think aloud for 3 minutes** about the essay question: *"Some people believe that economic growth should always take priority over environmental protection. To what extent do you agree?"* Speak your ideas into a voice recorder or phone. This activates your thinking, generates vocabulary, and mirrors the planning stage of a C1 speaking task.
- **Resource:** Your phone's voice recorder app
- **⏱️ Time:** 20 minutes

---

#### 👂 Listening — *Listening for Argument Structure*
- **Activity:** Listen to a **BBC Radio 4 debate or discussion** (e.g. *The Moral Maze* or *Any Questions?*). Focus specifically on **how speakers structure their arguments**: How do they introduce a point? How do they concede to the other side? How do they conclude? Take brief notes on the **argumentative moves** you notice, not just the content.
- **Resource:** [BBC Radio 4 – The Moral Maze](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b006qk11) | [BBC Sounds](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sounds)
- **⏱️ Time:** 30 minutes

---

#### 🔄 Mediation — *Balancing Perspectives*
- **Activity:** Read **two short opinion pieces** on the same topic (one for, one against) from The Guardian's opinion section. Write a **120-word balanced mediation text** that presents both perspectives fairly, without expressing your own opinion. Use language like: *"Proponents argue that...", "Critics, however, contend that...", "Both sides acknowledge..."*
- **Resource:** [The Guardian – Opinion](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree)
- **⏱️ Time:** 30 minutes

---

#### ✍️ Writing — *Full Opinion Essay Draft* ⭐ *Main Task of the Week*
- **Activity:** Write a **full opinion essay** (220–260 words) responding to: *"Some people believe that economic growth should always take priority over environmental protection. To what extent do you agree?"* Structure your essay with: a clear **introduction** (state the issue + your position), **two body paragraphs** (each with a main point, development, and example), and a **conclusion** (restate position + broader implication). Don't edit as you write — get the ideas down first.
- **Resource:** [British Council – Essay Writing Guide](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/skills/writing/c1-writing) | [Cambridge English Sample Essays](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org)
- **⏱️ Time:** 50 minutes

---

#### 📖 Reading — *Essay Model Analysis*
- **Activity:** Find a **model C1 opinion essay** online (Cambridge English provides samples). Read it carefully and answer these questions in writing: What is the thesis? How does each paragraph connect to it? What **linking devices** are used? What makes the vocabulary C1 rather than B2? This analytical reading directly improves your own writing.
- **Resource:** [Cambridge English – Sample Writing Tasks](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/advanced/preparation/) | [British Council Writing Samples](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/skills/writing)
- **⏱️ Time:** 25 minutes

### 🗓️ Thursday — *Vocabulary Expansion & Listening Detail*

**Daily Focus:** Precise vocabulary use + listening for specific information

---

#### 🗣️ Speaking — *Paraphrasing Under Pressure*
- **Activity:** Open any paragraph from a **news article**. Read one sentence, then immediately **paraphrase it aloud** without looking at it again. Do this for 8–10 sentences. This builds the paraphrasing skill essential for C1 speaking tasks, where you must express ideas flexibly when you can't recall the exact word you want.
- **Resource:** [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com) | [BBC News](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news)
- **⏱️ Time:** 25 minutes

---

#### 👂 Listening — *Listening for Specific Detail*
- **Activity:** Use a **Cambridge English listening practice test** (Part 2 or Part 3 style). Focus on listening for **specific information, numbers, names, and qualifications** — the kind of detail that distinguishes a strong C1 listener. After completing the task, replay the audio and identify **exactly where** each answer was stated. Understanding *why* an answer is correct is as important as getting it right.
- **Resource:** [Cambridge English – Free Practice Tests](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/learning-english/activities-for-learners/?skill=listening) | [British Council – C1 Listening](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/skills/listening/c1-listening)
- **⏱️ Time:** 40 minutes

---

#### 🔄 Mediation — *Simplifying Complex Information*
- **Activity:** Read a **short academic or scientific article** (try BBC Future or a Wikipedia article on a complex topic). Your task: write a **100-word plain-English explanation** of the main concept, as if explaining it to a 16-year-old with no specialist knowledge. This tests your ability to **adapt register and simplify without losing accuracy** — a key mediation skill.
- **Resource:** [BBC Future – Science & Technology](https://www.bbc.com/future/columns/science-focus)
- **⏱️ Time:** 25 minutes

---

#### ✍️ Writing — *Self-Editing Your Essay*
- **Activity:** Return to your **Wednesday essay draft**. Now edit it with fresh eyes using this checklist: ✅ Is every sentence grammatically accurate? ✅ Have you used at least 3 different complex structures? ✅ Is the vocabulary varied and C1-appropriate (no repetition of basic words)? ✅ Are your paragraphs clearly linked? ✅ Does your conclusion go beyond simply repeating the introduction? Rewrite any weak sections.
- **Resource:** [Grammarly (free version)](https://www.grammarly.com) *(use as a second opinion, not a crutch)* | [Ludwig.guru](https://ludwig.guru) *(for checking natural phrasing)*
- **⏱️ Time:** 35 minutes

---

#### 📖 Reading — *Collocation & Phrase Mining*
- **Activity:** Read an article from **The Economist or Financial Times** (free articles available). Focus on **verb-noun and adjective-noun collocations** — the combinations that make writing sound natural and sophisticated. Examples: *"pose a significant challenge", "yield tangible results", "exert considerable influence"*. Collect **8 strong collocations** and write a sentence using each one.
- **Resource:** [The Economist – Open Articles](https://www.economist.com) | [Financial Times – Free Articles](https://www.ft.com) | [Oxford Collocations Dictionary Online](https://www.freecollocation.com)
- **⏱️ Time:** 30 minutes

- **Activity:** Read your **edited essay aloud** as if presenting it to an audience. Then, without notes, give a **2-minute spoken response** to the same essay question — but this time, argue the **opposite position** from your essay. This forces you to think flexibly and use language you've been building all week. Record yourself and listen back critically.

- **Activity:** Listen to one episode of a **high-quality English podcast** on a topic you enjoy. This is **extensive listening** — don't take notes, don't pause, just listen. The goal is fluency and naturalness, not analysis. Notice how native speakers use **hedging language, fillers, and discourse markers** in natural conversation.
- **Resource:** [Podcasts in English – C1/C2](https://www.podcastsinenglish.com) | [6 Minute English – BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/english/features/6-minute-english) | [Freakonomics Radio](https://freakonomics.com/archive/)

Write a **brief reflection** (50 words) on what mediation skill you want to improve next week. This metacognitive habit is what separates good learners from great ones.
- **Resource:** Your own notes from the week
- **⏱️ Time:** 20 minutes

#### ✍️ Writing — *Vocabulary Journal Update*

Organise this week's new words and phrases by **topic** (e.g. Environment, Technology, Society) and **function** (e.g. Expressing contrast, Hedging, Emphasising). Aim to have at least **20 new items** recorded with example sentences. A well-organised vocabulary journal is one of the most powerful tools a C1 learner can have.
- **Resource:** [Quizlet](https://quizlet.com) *(create digital flashcard sets)* | [Notion](https://www.notion.so) *(for a structured digital journal)*
- **⏱️ Time:** 25 minutes

#### 📖 Reading — *Timed Reading Practice*
- **Activity:** Choose a **medium-length article** (600–800 words) from BBC News or The Guardian. Set a timer for **8 minutes** and read it once. Without looking back, write down: the main argument, three key supporting points, and the author's overall tone. Then check your notes against the article. This builds the **reading speed and comprehension** needed for timed exam conditions.
- **Resource:** [BBC News](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news) | [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com)
- **⏱️ Time:** 25 minutes

## 🏖️ Weekend Consolidation Tasks

### Task 1 — *Grammar in the Wild* (Saturday | ~45 minutes)
Read any long-form article or essay online and **hunt for examples of the complex structures** you studied this week: inversions, cleft sentences, mixed conditionals, passive reporting verbs. Copy 5 examples into your notebook and annotate why the author chose that structure. Then write **3 original sentences** imitating each structure in a new context.

### Task 2 — *Essay Reflection & Rewrite* (Sunday | ~50 minutes)
Read your **final edited essay** one more time with completely fresh eyes. Ask yourself honestly: *Does this sound like C1? Would an examiner be impressed?* Identify the **one weakest paragraph** and rewrite it from scratch, aiming for greater precision, sophistication, and coherence. Compare the two versions and note what improved.

## 💡 Three C1-Specific Tips for This Week

### Tip 1 — *Upgrade Your Sentence Starters*
One of the clearest signals of C1 writing is **how you begin sentences**. Replace weak openers like *"I think..."* or *"There are many reasons..."* with sophisticated alternatives:
- *"It is widely acknowledged that..."*
- *"What is particularly striking is..."*
- *"Not only does this suggest..., but it also..."*
- *"Central to this debate is the question of..."*

Practise these until they feel natural — they will transform the register of your writing immediately.

---

### Tip 2 — *Listen for Chunks, Not Words*
When doing listening tasks this week, train yourself to process **chunks of language** rather than individual words. Native speakers don't pause between every word — they group words into meaningful units. For example, *"it's worth bearing in mind that"* is one chunk, not six separate words. When you catch a useful chunk, write it down whole and practise using it as a unit.

---

### Tip 3 — *Your Essay Is an Argument, Not a List*
A common B2-to-C1 transition mistake is writing essays that **list points** rather than **build an argument**. At C1, each paragraph should connect logically to the next, and your conclusion should feel **inevitable** given what came before — not just a summary. Ask yourself after each paragraph: *"How does this move my argument forward?"* If it doesn't, revise it.
