## 📌 Acknowledging Your Current Challenges

Since you're stepping into **Week 2** without flagged weak areas, that's actually useful information in itself — it likely means you're still in the *diagnostic phase*, figuring out where the cracks are. That's completely normal, and Week 2 is the **perfect moment to find them before they become habits**.

At C1 level, the most universally underestimated challenges are:

- **Lexical precision** — knowing a word isn't enough; you need to deploy it correctly in context
- **Discourse coherence** — connecting ideas across paragraphs, not just within sentences
- **Listening for implied meaning** — C1 audio rarely states things directly
- **Writing register control** — shifting fluently between formal, semi-formal, and neutral tones

These aren't beginner gaps. They're the exact areas that separate B2 performance from genuine C1 mastery, and **Week 2 is when you should start stress-testing yourself in all of them.**

## 🛠️ Five Specific Improvement Strategies for Week 2

### 1. 🔍 Run a Vocabulary Audit on Your Own Writing
**Why it matters:** C1 candidates frequently recycle the same 200 words under exam pressure, which caps their score regardless of grammar accuracy.

**Today's micro-task:** Write 10 sentences on any topic. Then go back and highlight every word you consider "safe" or "default" — words like *good, important, show, use, big*. Replace each one with a more precise or sophisticated alternative using a collocation dictionary (e.g., *odict.net* or *Macmillan Collocations*). Notice how the meaning sharpens.

---

### 2. 🎧 Practice "Inference Listening" with Podcasts
**Why it matters:** C1 listening tasks (especially Part 4 of CAE) test your ability to understand *attitude, opinion, and implication* — not just facts.

**Today's micro-task:** Listen to any 3-minute segment of a native-speaker podcast (BBC Radio 4, *The Guardian's* audio content, or *Thinking Allowed* work well). After listening, write down:
- What the speaker *explicitly* said
- What they *implied* or *felt* but didn't state directly
- One word or phrase you didn't fully catch — look it up immediately

Do this **without subtitles first**, then verify.

---

### 3. ✍️ Master the "Signpost Sentence" in Writing
**Why it matters:** C1 writing is assessed heavily on **coherence and cohesion**. Examiners read fast — your paragraph transitions need to do real work, not just decorate.

**Today's micro-task:** Take any paragraph you've written recently and add or rewrite its **opening sentence** so that it does three things simultaneously:
1. Links back to the previous idea
2. Introduces the new point
3. Signals the *direction* of the argument (contrast, addition, concession, etc.)

Example of a weak opener: *"Another point is time management."*
Example of a strong signpost: *"While financial investment is undeniably critical, the more immediate obstacle organisations face is arguably the misallocation of existing time."*

Practice writing five of these today on different topics.

---

### 4. 📖 Read One Article "Like an Examiner"
**Why it matters:** C1 reading tasks (especially multiple matching and gapped text) require you to understand **text architecture** — how a writer builds and organises meaning across a whole piece.

**Today's micro-task:** Read one article from *The Economist*, *The Atlantic*, or *New Scientist*. As you read, draw a simple map:
- What is the **thesis** (stated or implied)?
- Where does the writer **concede** a counterargument?
- What is the **pivot point** — where does the argument shift?

This trains the structural awareness that gapped-text tasks directly test.

---

### 5. 🗣️ Record a 90-Second Spoken Response Daily
**Why it matters:** Speaking fluency at C1 isn't about speed — it's about **sustained, organised speech** with minimal self-correction. This skill degrades without daily activation.

**Today's micro-task:** Set a timer for 90 seconds. Choose a prompt (e.g., *"Is remote work ultimately better for productivity?"*). Speak continuously, record yourself, then listen back and note:
- Did you use any **discourse markers** to organise your points? (*Furthermore, That said, What's particularly interesting is...*)
- Did you **self-correct mid-sentence** more than twice? (A C1 red flag)
- Was your final sentence a **clear conclusion** or did you trail off?

Repeat tomorrow with a new prompt.

## ⚠️ One Critical Mistake to Avoid This Week

### ❌ Treating Vocabulary Learning as a List Exercise

**The mistake:** Many C1 students write down new words in lists with translations or single definitions, then feel they've "learned" them.

**Why it fails at C1:** Examiners don't test whether you *know* a word — they test whether you can use it **accurately in context**, with the right collocation, register, and grammatical pattern. A word learned in isolation is a word you'll misuse under pressure.

**The correction:** Every new word you encounter this week, record it in a **full sentence you wrote yourself**, note its **collocates** (what words naturally sit beside it), and tag its **register** (formal / informal / neutral). For example:

> ❌ *List entry:* "mitigate = reduce"
> ✅ *Rich entry:* "mitigate the impact/risk/effects of [formal] — *The new policy was designed to mitigate the long-term economic effects of the crisis.*"

This takes 60 extra seconds per word and **doubles retention**.
