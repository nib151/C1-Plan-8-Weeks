# 🎯 Week 5: Advanced Production
### *Formal & Semi-Formal Writing · Debate-Style Speaking · Nuanced Vocabulary in Context*

---

## 📌 Weekly Theme & Learning Objectives

This week marks a significant leap forward — you're moving from **understanding** C1 English to **producing** it with precision and sophistication. The focus is on crafting language that is contextually appropriate, stylistically varied, and rhetorically effective. By the end of this week, you will be able to distinguish between formal and semi-formal registers in writing, construct and defend arguments with nuance in spoken English, and deploy advanced vocabulary not just accurately, but *strategically*.

## 🗓️ Day-by-Day Breakdown

---

### 📅 Monday — *Register Mastery & Formal Writing*

**Daily Focus:** Understanding how register shapes meaning — and writing a formal report or proposal with confidence.

---

#### ✍️ Writing — *Formal Report or Proposal* `⏱️ 50 minutes`
- **Task:** Write a **250–300 word formal report** recommending improvements to a public service (e.g., local transport, a community library, or a university facility).
- Use impersonal constructions: *"It is recommended that…"*, *"The findings suggest…"*, *"Considerable improvement could be achieved by…"*
- Include: a brief introduction, two or three clearly headed sections, and a conclusion with recommendations.
- **Focus on:** avoiding contractions, using passive voice purposefully, and maintaining a consistent formal tone throughout.
- 📎 **Resource:** [British Council — Formal Writing Guide](https://www.britishcouncil.org/english/learn-online) — search for "formal writing C1" in their skills section.

---

#### 📖 Reading — *Register Comparison Exercise* `⏱️ 35 minutes`
- **Task:** Find **two articles on the same topic** — one from *The Guardian* (broadsheet, formal-to-neutral) and one from a tabloid or blog (informal). Compare how the same information is presented differently.
- Identify: vocabulary choices, sentence length, use of hedging language, and tone.
- Write **8–10 bullet points** noting specific linguistic differences you observe.
- 📎 **Resource:** [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com) — try the *Education* or *Environment* sections for accessible formal writing.

---

#### 🎧 Listening — *Formal Spoken English in Context* `⏱️ 30 minutes`
- **Task:** Watch a **BBC Radio 4 programme excerpt** or a formal panel discussion on YouTube (search: *"BBC Radio 4 In Our Time"* — any episode).
- As you listen, note **5 phrases** the speakers use to introduce, qualify, or challenge an idea formally (e.g., *"One might argue…"*, *"That said, however…"*, *"The evidence points rather to…"*).
- 📎 **Resource:** [BBC Radio 4 — In Our Time](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b006qykl/episodes/downloads) — free podcast episodes available.

---

#### 🗣️ Speaking — *Introducing Formal Argumentation* `⏱️ 25 minutes`
- **Task:** Record yourself giving a **2-minute structured monologue** on the following prompt:
  > *"Some people believe that universities should focus exclusively on academic subjects rather than offering vocational training. To what extent do you agree?"*
- Use formal discourse markers: *"In the first instance…"*, *"A compelling counterargument would be…"*, *"On balance, it seems evident that…"*
- Play it back and check: Did you use any informal fillers (*"like," "you know," "kind of"*)? Replace them mentally with C1 alternatives.
- 📎 **Resource:** [Cambridge English — Speaking Sample Videos (C1)](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/advanced/preparation/) — watch the examiner commentary for register tips.

---

#### 🔄 Mediation — *Summarising a Formal Text for a Different Audience* `⏱️ 25 minutes`
- **Task:** Take a short formal text (e.g., a government press release or academic abstract — find one via [GOV.UK News](https://www.gov.uk/search/news-and-communications)) and **rewrite the key points** in semi-formal English for a friend who is intelligent but not a specialist.
- Aim for **100–120 words**. You are not simplifying the ideas — you are **re-registering** them.
- Ask yourself: What can I keep? What must I rephrase? What context do I need to add?

### 📅 Tuesday — *Debate-Style Speaking & Persuasive Language*

**Daily Focus:** Constructing and defending arguments under pressure — the hallmark of C1 spoken production.

---

#### 🗣️ Speaking — *Debate Simulation* `⏱️ 45 minutes`
- **Task:** Choose one of the following controversial statements and prepare **both sides** of the argument (5 minutes planning), then record yourself debating *against your own initial position* for **3 minutes**:
  > *"Social media has done more harm than good to public discourse."*
  > *"Economic growth should always take priority over environmental concerns."*
- This challenges you to use language of **concession and rebuttal**: *"While it is true that…, this argument fails to account for…"*, *"Proponents of this view overlook the fact that…"*
- 📎 **Resource:** [TED Talks — search "debate" or "argument"](https://www.ted.com) — watch how speakers structure persuasive arguments and steal their best phrases.

---

#### 📖 Reading — *Argumentative Text Analysis* `⏱️ 35 minutes`
- **Task:** Read a **comment piece or opinion column** from *The Guardian* or *The Economist* (free articles available online). As you read:
  - Underline the **thesis statement**
  - Circle all **hedging language** (*"arguably," "it could be contended," "there is a strong case for"*)
  - Mark where the writer **acknowledges the opposing view** before refuting it
- This is the architecture of C1 argumentation — internalise it.
- 📎 **Resource:** [The Economist — Open Access Articles](https://www.economist.com) or [The Guardian — Opinion](https://www.theguardian.com/uk/commentisfree)

---

#### 🎧 Listening — *Debate & Discussion Comprehension* `⏱️ 30 minutes`
- **Task:** Watch a **BBC Newsnight** or **Intelligence Squared** debate clip on YouTube (search: *"Intelligence Squared debate"* — full debates are free).
- Focus on **how speakers interrupt politely**, hold the floor, and signal disagreement without being rude.
- Write down **6 functional phrases** for: interrupting, agreeing partially, and strongly disagreeing.
- 📎 **Resource:** [Intelligence Squared YouTube Channel](https://www.youtube.com/@intelligencesquared)

---

#### ✍️ Writing — *Semi-Formal Email of Complaint or Proposal* `⏱️ 40 minutes`
- **Task:** Write a **semi-formal email (220–260 words)** to a university department head proposing a new student initiative (e.g., a mentoring programme, a sustainability project, a language exchange scheme).
- Semi-formal means: polite but not stiff, professional but not cold. Use first person, but avoid slang.
- Include: a clear purpose statement, two or three developed points, and a forward-looking close.
- 📎 **Resource:** [BBC Learning English — Writing Skills](https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/english/features/writing-skills)

---

#### 🔄 Mediation — *Oral Summary of a Debate* `⏱️ 20 minutes`
- **Task:** After your listening task above, record a **90-second spoken summary** of the debate you watched — as if you are explaining it to someone who wasn't there.
- You must represent **both sides fairly** and give your own brief evaluation at the end.
- This mirrors the C1 mediation task where you synthesise and relay information across perspectives.

### 📅 Wednesday — *Nuanced Vocabulary in Context*

**Daily Focus:** Moving beyond "good" vocabulary to *precise*, *contextually aware*, and *stylistically sophisticated* word choices.

---

#### 📖 Reading — *Vocabulary in Context Deep Dive* `⏱️ 40 minutes`
- **Task:** Read a **long-form article** (800+ words) from *The Atlantic*, *Prospect Magazine*, or *The Guardian Long Read* section.
- Identify **10 words or phrases** you recognise but wouldn't naturally produce yourself. For each one:
  - Write the sentence it appears in
  - Write your own new example sentence using it
  - Note any collocations (what words typically appear with it?)
- 📎 **Resource:** [The Guardian — The Long Read](https://www.theguardian.com/news/series/the-long-read)

---

#### 🗣️ Speaking — *Vocabulary Activation Challenge* `⏱️ 30 minutes`
- **Task:** Take **5 of the 10 words** you collected in your reading task and record yourself using each one naturally in a **spoken response** to this prompt:
  > *"What are the most significant challenges facing young people in today's society?"*
- The goal is **integration** — the words should feel natural, not forced. If they sound awkward, try again.
- Time yourself: aim for a fluent 2.5–3 minute response.

---

#### 🎧 Listening — *Vocabulary in Authentic Speech* `⏱️ 35 minutes`
- **Task:** Listen to a **TED Talk** on a topic you find genuinely interesting (this matters — engagement improves retention).
- First listen: for general comprehension.
- Second listen (or read the transcript): identify **8 advanced words or collocations** the speaker uses.
- Note how they are pronounced — stress, connected speech, intonation.
- 📎 **Resource:** [TED Talks with Transcripts](https://www.ted.com/talks) — click "Transcript" under any talk.

---

#### ✍️ Writing — *Vocabulary-Focused Paragraph Rewriting* `⏱️ 35 minutes`
- **Task:** Take a paragraph you wrote earlier this week (from Monday or Tuesday) and **rewrite it twice**:
  - Version 1: Replace at least **5 words** with more precise or sophisticated alternatives
  - Version 2: Change the **register** (if it was formal, make it semi-formal, or vice versa)
- Compare all three versions. Which reads best? Why?
- 📎 **Resource:** [Merriam-Webster Thesaurus](https://www.merriam-webster.com/thesaurus) + [Ozdic Collocation Dictionary](https://ozdic.com) — essential tools for C1 writers.

---

#### 🔄 Mediation — *Glossing Specialist Language* `⏱️ 25 minutes`
- **Task:** Find a short text with **specialist or technical vocabulary** (e.g., a science news article from [BBC Science](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/science_and_environment) or a legal summary from a news site).
- Your task: **mediate it in writing** for a general audience in 100–130 words, replacing jargon with accessible but still precise language.
- This is a core C1 mediation competency — making complex language *accessible without dumbing it down*.

### 📅 Thursday — *Integrated Skills Under Pressure*

**Daily Focus:** Combining skills in realistic, exam-like conditions — building stamina and fluency simultaneously.

---

#### 🎧 Listening — *Extended Listening with Note-Taking* `⏱️ 40 minutes`
- **Task:** Listen to a **30-minute podcast episode** from *BBC In Our Time*, *Freakonomics Radio*, or *The Inquiry (BBC World Service)* — all free.
- Take structured notes using the **Cornell Method**: main ideas on the right, key terms on the left, summary at the bottom.
- After listening, write a **5-sentence summary** without looking at your notes first, then check.
- 📎 **Resource:** [BBC World Service — The Inquiry Podcast](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p029399x/episodes/downloads)

---

#### 🗣️ Speaking — *Timed Debate Response* `⏱️ 35 minutes`
- **Task:** This is a **timed challenge**. Set a timer for **2 minutes preparation**, then record a **3-minute spoken argument** on this prompt with NO further pausing:
  > *"The arts should receive the same level of government funding as the sciences."*
- Evaluate your recording against these criteria:
  - ✅ Did you use at least 3 discourse markers?
  - ✅ Did you acknowledge a counterargument?
  - ✅ Did you avoid repetition of key vocabulary?
  - ✅ Was your conclusion distinct from your introduction?

---

#### 📖 Reading — *Timed Reading Practice* `⏱️ 30 minutes`
- **Task:** Find a **600–800 word article** and set a timer for **12 minutes** to read it and answer these self-generated questions:
  - What is the writer's main argument?
  - What evidence do they use?
  - What is implied but not directly stated?
  - What vocabulary choices reveal the writer's attitude?
- This trains the **inferencing and critical reading** skills essential at C1.
- 📎 **Resource:** [Prospect Magazine](https://www.prospectmagazine.co.uk) — excellent C1-level articles, many free.

---

#### ✍️ Writing — *Timed Essay Draft* `⏱️ 45 minutes`
- **Task:** Write a **full discursive essay (280–320 words)** in **40 minutes** (leave 5 minutes for review) on:
  > *"To what extent is it the responsibility of individuals, rather than governments, to address climate change?"*
- Use the **4-paragraph structure**: Introduction (thesis) → Argument 1 with evidence → Argument 2 / counterargument → Conclusion (balanced judgement).
- After the timer, spend the remaining 5 minutes **proofreading only for register consistency and vocabulary precision** — not content.

---

#### 🔄 Mediation — *Written-to-Spoken Mediation* `⏱️ 20 minutes`
- **Task:** Take your essay from the writing task above and **present its key arguments verbally** in a 90-second spoken summary — as if you are briefing a colleague who needs to understand the issue quickly.
- You may not read from the essay. Use your own words.
- This bridges written and spoken production — a sophisticated C1 skill.

### 📅 Friday — *Consolidation, Reflection & Refinement*

**Daily Focus:** Reviewing the week's output, refining your best work, and cementing new language.

---

#### ✍️ Writing — *Revision and Editing Workshop* `⏱️ 40 minutes`
- **Task:** Select the **best piece of writing** you produced this week. Now edit it rigorously:
  - Check for **register consistency** throughout
  - Replace any **weak or repeated vocabulary**
  - Ensure **cohesion devices** are varied (*furthermore, nevertheless, in contrast, as a consequence*)
  - Read it aloud — does it *sound* like C1 English?
- Produce a clean final version. This habit of revision is what separates B2 writers from C1 writers.

- Then listen back specifically for **repair strategies**: How do you handle hesitation? Do you use fillers or do you use C1 stalling phrases like *"That's an interesting point to consider…"* or *"What I'm trying to convey is…"*?
- Write down **3 repair phrases** you want to use more naturally.
- 📎 **Resource:** [BBC Learning English — Pronunciation & Fluency](https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/english/features/pronunciation)

## 💡 Three C1-Specific Tips for This Week

---

### 💡 Tip 1: *Register Is Not Just Vocabulary — It's Architecture*
Many students think switching register means swapping informal words for formal ones. At C1, it goes much deeper. Formal writing uses **longer, more complex sentences**, **passive constructions**, **impersonal subjects** (*"It is widely acknowledged…"*), and **nominalisation** (turning verbs into nouns: *"investigate" → "the investigation of"*). When you edit your writing this week, look beyond individual words and examine your **sentence structures and grammatical choices**.

---

### 💡 Tip 2: *In Debate-Style Speaking, Silence Is Your Enemy — But Fillers Are Worse*
When you're under pressure in a speaking task, the temptation is to fill silence with *"um," "like," "you know."* At C1, you are expected to manage hesitation **strategically**. Practise these stalling phrases until they feel automatic:
- *"That's a nuanced question — let me consider both sides…"*
- *"What I find particularly compelling about this issue is…"*
- *"It's worth distinguishing here between two different aspects…"*
These phrases buy you thinking time *while demonstrating sophistication* — a double win.

---

### 💡 Tip 3: *Collect Language in Chunks, Not Just Words*
The biggest vocabulary mistake C1 learners make is learning words in isolation. Native speakers don't think in words — they think in **chunks**: *"raise serious concerns," "a marked improvement," "broadly speaking," "it stands to reason that."* This week, every time you record a new word, force yourself to also record **the two or three words most commonly found beside it**. Your writing and speaking will immediately sound more natural and sophisticated.
