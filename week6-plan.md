### 🗓️ MONDAY — *Reading Under the Clock*

**Daily Focus:** Timed reading strategies and multiple-choice precision

---

#### 📖 Reading — *60 minutes*
**Activity: Timed Multiple-Choice & Gapped Text Practice**

- Set a timer for **20 minutes** and complete one C1-level multiple-choice reading task. Use texts from Cambridge C1 Advanced sample papers.
- Focus on the **elimination strategy**: for each question, actively cross out two obviously wrong answers before choosing between the remaining two.
- After the timer, spend **15 minutes reviewing** every question — including the ones you got right. Ask yourself: *"Why is this answer correct and not the others?"*
- Spend the final **25 minutes** on a gapped text task (sentences removed from a paragraph). Practise identifying **cohesive devices** (however, nevertheless, as a result) and **pronoun references** as clues for correct placement.

**🔗 Resources:**
- [Cambridge C1 Advanced Sample Papers](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/advanced/preparation/) — free downloadable reading tasks
- [British Council Reading C1 Practice](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/skills/reading/c1-reading)

---

#### 🎧 Listening — *45 minutes*
**Activity: Structured Note-Taking Introduction**

- Listen to a **BBC Radio 4 documentary clip** (8–12 minutes) on any topic of general interest.
- Before listening, **prepare a simple note-taking grid** with three columns: *Main Idea / Supporting Detail / Speaker's Attitude.*
- Listen **twice**: first for gist and main ideas, second to fill in supporting details and identify tone/attitude.
- Review your notes and write **three summary sentences** capturing the key content.

**🔗 Resources:**
- [BBC Radio 4 In Our Time Podcast](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b006qykl/episodes/downloads) — rich, academic-register audio
- [BBC Learning English — Listening Practice](https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/english/features/6-minute-english)

---

#### 🗣️ Speaking — *30 minutes*
**Activity: Long Turn Timing Drill**

- Choose **two contrasting images** from a Google Image search (e.g., two different working environments, two social situations).
- Set a timer for **exactly 2 minutes** and deliver your long turn aloud — compare, speculate, and give a personal perspective.
- Record yourself on your phone.
- Play it back and evaluate: Did you fill the full 2 minutes? Did you use **speculative language** (*it would appear that, this might suggest, one could argue*)? Did you avoid long pauses?
- Repeat with a second pair of images, aiming to improve on your weak points.

**🔗 Resources:**
- [Cambridge English — Speaking Sample Videos](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/advanced/preparation/)

---

#### ✍️ Writing — *45 minutes*
**Activity: Timed Essay Planning Sprint**

- You will **not write a full essay today** — instead, focus entirely on the planning phase under pressure.
- Choose a C1 essay prompt (e.g., *"Some people believe that technology has made modern life more complicated rather than simpler. Discuss."*)
- Set a timer for **8 minutes** and produce a full essay plan: thesis, three body paragraph topics, one counterargument, conclusion direction.
- Then spend **37 minutes** writing only the **introduction and first body paragraph**, focusing on a strong thesis statement and a well-developed argument with evidence.
- Evaluate: Is your thesis **specific and arguable** (not just a statement of fact)?

**🔗 Resources:**
- [British Council — Essay Writing C1](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/skills/writing)
- [Cambridge English Write & Improve](https://writeandimprove.com/) — free AI feedback tool

---

#### 🔄 Mediation — *30 minutes*
**Activity: Evaluative Summary from Two Sources**

- Read **two short opinion pieces** on the same topic (e.g., remote working) — one broadly positive, one critical.
- Write a **150-word mediation text** for a third party (e.g., a manager making a policy decision) that:
  - Summarises both perspectives fairly
  - Evaluates which argument is better supported with evidence
  - Ends with a **reasoned recommendation**
- This is the *evaluation* layer of mediation — moving beyond summary into critical judgement.

**🔗 Resources:**
- [The Guardian Opinion Section](https://www.theguardian.com/uk/commentisfree) — pairs of contrasting opinion pieces

### 🗓️ TUESDAY — *Listening with Precision*

**Daily Focus:** Exam-style listening strategies and note-taking under timed conditions

---

#### 🎧 Listening — *60 minutes*
**Activity: C1 Listening Task Simulation**

- Complete a **full C1 Advanced Listening paper** (Parts 1–4) under timed exam conditions. Do not pause or rewind — simulate the real exam experience.
- Part 1 (multiple choice extracts): Focus on **distractor awareness** — the exam deliberately includes words from wrong answers in the audio.
- Part 2 (sentence completion): Write answers of **1–3 words only**; practise predicting the word type (noun, adjective, number) before each gap.
- After completing, review all incorrect answers and identify your **error pattern**: mishearing, distraction by false cognates, or missing attitude/opinion questions.

**🔗 Resources:**
- [Cambridge C1 Listening Sample Tests](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/advanced/preparation/)
- [British Council Listening C1](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/skills/listening/c1-listening)

---

#### 📖 Reading — *45 minutes*
**Activity: Skimming and Scanning Speed Drill**

- Take a long article from The Guardian or The Economist (800–1,000 words).
- **Skim read** the entire article in **90 seconds** — read only the first sentence of each paragraph. Then write down what you think the article is about.
- Now **scan** for five specific pieces of information (e.g., statistics, names, dates, contrasting opinions) in under **3 minutes**.
- Finally, read the full article normally and check how accurate your skim/scan predictions were.
- Reflect: Which paragraph structures helped or hindered your skimming?

**🔗 Resources:**
- [The Economist — Free Articles](https://www.economist.com/)
- [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/)

---

#### 🗣️ Speaking — *30 minutes*
**Activity: Collaborative Task Simulation (Solo Version)**

- Choose a scenario (e.g., *"A company wants to improve employee wellbeing. Discuss five possible initiatives and decide which two would be most effective."*)
- Speak aloud for **3 minutes**, working through the options as if with a partner — use **negotiation language**: *"I think we should prioritise… / That's a valid point, but… / What if we considered…"*
- Record yourself and check: Are you **inviting opinions** (even hypothetically)? Are you **building on ideas** rather than just listing them?

---

#### ✍️ Writing — *50 minutes*
**Activity: Formal Report — Structure and Register Drill**

- Write a **formal report** (220–260 words) on the following prompt: *"You have been asked to write a report for your college principal on the effectiveness of current study support services and to make recommendations for improvement."*
- Focus specifically on **report conventions**: clear headings (*Introduction, Findings, Recommendations*), impersonal passive constructions (*It was found that… / It is recommended that…*), and formal vocabulary.
- Use Cambridge Write & Improve to get instant feedback on register and accuracy.

**🔗 Resources:**
- [Cambridge Write & Improve](https://writeandimprove.com/)
- [British Council — Formal Writing Guide](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/skills/writing/c1-writing)

---

#### 🔄 Mediation — *30 minutes*
**Activity: Spoken Mediation — Relay and Evaluate**

- Watch a **3–4 minute TED Talk excerpt** (choose a talk with a clear argument).
- Pause after watching and **speak aloud for 90 seconds**, relaying the speaker's key points to an imaginary colleague who hasn't seen it.
- Then add **30 seconds of your own evaluation**: *"I found this argument convincing/unconvincing because…"*
- Record yourself. Check: Did you use **reporting language** (*The speaker argues that… / According to the presenter…*)? Did your evaluation go beyond personal preference into reasoned critique?

**🔗 Resources:**
- [TED Talks](https://www.ted.com/talks) — filter by topic and length

### 🗓️ WEDNESDAY — *Writing Under Pressure*

**Daily Focus:** Timed writing production and register control

---

#### ✍️ Writing — *75 minutes*
**Activity: Full Timed Writing Task (Part 2 — Letter/Proposal)**

- This is your **main session today**. Set a strict timer for **45 minutes** and write a complete C1 Part 2 task.
- Prompt: *"You have seen an advertisement asking for contributions to an international magazine on the theme of 'What makes a great city to live in?' Write an article (220–260 words) sharing your views and giving examples."*
- **Planning phase (8 minutes):** Brainstorm, select your angle, plan paragraph structure.
- **Writing phase (37 minutes):** Write the full article. Do not stop to edit — keep moving forward.
- **Review phase (remaining time after 45 min):** Spend the final **30 minutes** reviewing your work against the C1 marking criteria: task achievement, coherence, vocabulary range, grammatical accuracy.

**🔗 Resources:**
- [Cambridge C1 Writing Assessment Criteria](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/images/167804-cambridge-english-advanced-information-for-candidates.pdf)
- [Cambridge Write & Improve](https://writeandimprove.com/)

#### 📖 Reading — *45 minutes*
**Activity: Cross-Text Multiple Matching**

- Complete a **cross-text multiple matching task** (C1 Reading Part 4 format): four short texts on the same topic, with questions asking you to identify which writer expresses a particular view.
- Key strategy: **Paraphrase recognition** — the question will never use the exact words from the text. Practise identifying synonyms and conceptual matches.
- After completing, highlight every paraphrase pair you found (question language → text language).

**🔗 Resources:**
- [Cambridge C1 Sample Reading Papers](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/advanced/preparation/)

#### 🎧 Listening — *35 minutes*
**Activity: Attitude and Opinion Focus**

- Listen to a **debate or discussion podcast** (15–20 minutes) featuring two or more speakers with differing views.
- As you listen, note down **specific phrases** each speaker uses to express attitude: agreement, disagreement, hedging, emphasis.
- Create a vocabulary bank of **10 attitude-expressing phrases** you could use in your own speaking and writing.

**🔗 Resources:**
- [BBC Radio 4 — The Moral Maze](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b006qk11) — structured debate format

#### 🗣️ Speaking — *30 minutes*
**Activity: Structured Monologue — Exam Part 2 Drill**

- Choose a **C1 speaking card prompt** (available in Cambridge sample materials).
- Deliver your **2-minute long turn** three times in a row, each time with a different focus:
  - **Round 1:** Focus on content and ideas only
  - **Round 2:** Focus on using a wider range of vocabulary (no repeating the same adjective twice)
  - **Round 3:** Focus on natural fluency and intonation — no long pauses
- Record Round 3 and compare it to Round 1. Notice the improvement.

---

#### 🔄 Mediation — *25 minutes*
**Activity: Written Mediation — Adapting Register**

- Take a **complex academic paragraph** (from a journal article or The Economist) and rewrite it in **plain, accessible English** for a non-specialist audience (e.g., a community newsletter).
- Then rewrite the same content in **formal professional English** for a business report.
- Compare your two versions: What changed? What stayed the same? This builds your register flexibility — a key C1 mediation skill.

**🔗 Resources:**

#### 🗣️ Speaking — *60 minutes*
**Activity: Full Speaking Exam Simulation**

  - **Part 3 (4 min):** Collaborative task — speak through a decision-making scenario alone, using both sides of the argument
  - **Part 4 (5 min):** Answer abstract follow-up questions (e.g., *"Do you think society places too much value on academic achievement?"*)
- Record the entire session. Evaluate yourself on: **fluency, vocabulary range, grammatical complexity, and task completion.**

#### 🎧 Listening — *40 minutes*
**Activity: Note-Taking System Refinement**

- Listen to a **TED Talk** (12–15 minutes) on an academic or social topic.
- This time, use the **Cornell Note-Taking Method**: divide your page into a narrow left column (keywords/questions) and a wide right column (notes). Leave space at the bottom for a summary.
- After listening, **cover the right column** and use only your keywords to reconstruct the main points aloud.
- This technique directly mirrors what you need for C1 listening tasks where you must retain and process information quickly.

**🔗 Resources:**
- [TED Talks — Ideas Worth Spreading](https://www.ted.com/talks)

---

#### 📖 Reading — *40 minutes*
**Activity: Inference and Implication Practice**

- Read a **literary or opinion-based article** and focus on what is **implied but not stated directly**.
- After reading, write **five inference questions** for yourself (e.g., *"What does the writer's choice of the word 'merely' suggest about their attitude?"*) and answer them.
- This targets the higher-order comprehension skills tested in C1 reading — particularly in multiple-choice questions about writer's purpose and tone.

**🔗 Resources:**
- [The Guardian Long Reads](https://www.theguardian.com/news/series/the-long-read)
- [BBC Culture Articles](https://www.bbc.com/culture)

#### ✍️ Writing — *40 minutes*
**Activity: Error Analysis and Self-Correction**

- Take your **Monday or Tuesday writing task** and re-read it with fresh eyes.
- Using a colour-coding system, mark:
  - 🔴 **Red:** Grammar errors
  - 🟡 **Yellow:** Vocabulary that could be more precise or sophisticated
  - 🟢 **Green:** Sentences you're proud of — effective, complex, accurate
- Rewrite every red and yellow section. Aim to have **more green than red** by the end.
- This metacognitive editing process is one of the most powerful exam preparation techniques available.

#### 🔄 Mediation — *30 minutes*
**Activity: Spoken Mediation with Evaluation — Timed**

- Read a **400-word article** on a current issue (education, environment, technology).
- Set a timer for **3 minutes** and deliver a spoken mediation to an imaginary audience who hasn't read the article.
- Your mediation must include:
  - A brief context-setting opening
  - The article's main argument
  - One piece of supporting evidence
  - Your evaluation of the argument's strength
- Replay and check: Did you stay within time? Was your evaluation **reasoned**, not just opinionated?

#### ✍️ Writing — *40 minutes*
**Activity: Vocabulary and Cohesion Upgrade**

- Take any piece of writing from this week and focus exclusively on **cohesion and flow**.
- Check every paragraph transition: are you using a **variety of linking devices**? Create a checklist:
  - ✅ Contrast: *nevertheless, even so, that said, whilst*
  - ✅ Addition: *furthermore, what is more, in addition to this*
  - ✅ Cause/Effect: *consequently, as a result, this has led to*
  - ✅ Concession: *admittedly, granted, it must be acknowledged that*
- Rewrite any paragraph that uses the same connector more than once.

## 💡 Three C1-Specific Tips for Week 6

---

### 🔑 Tip 1: Learn to Love Distractors in Listening

In C1 listening tasks, **wrong answers are deliberately seductive** — they often contain words you actually heard in the audio. Train yourself to ask: *"Did I hear this word, or did I understand this idea?"* The correct answer paraphrases the audio; it rarely quotes it directly. This week, every time you get a listening question wrong, identify whether a distractor word misled you — and you'll start to see the pattern.

---

### 🔑 Tip 2: The 8-Minute Planning Rule for Writing

Never skip the planning phase, even under time pressure — **especially** under time pressure. Eight minutes of planning saves you fifteen minutes of confused writing and produces a far more coherent text. A C1 examiner can tell within the first paragraph whether a candidate planned their essay. Your plan doesn't need to be elaborate: a thesis, three topic sentences, and a conclusion direction is enough to keep you on track for the full 45 minutes.

---

### 🔑 Tip 3: Mediation Is Not a Summary — It's a Service

The most common mistake in C1 mediation tasks is producing a **sophisticated paraphrase** and calling it mediation. True mediation means you are **serving a specific audience with a specific need**. Before you write or speak, ask: *"Who is my audience? What do they need to know? What can I leave out? What must I evaluate for them?"* When you answer these questions first, your mediation becomes purposeful — and that purposefulness is exactly what examiners reward at C1 level.
