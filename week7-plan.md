### 📅 Monday — Speaking & Listening Under Pressure

**Daily Focus:** Simulating exam-condition performance in your two most time-sensitive skills.

---

#### 🗣️ Speaking — *45 minutes*

**Activity: Full Mock Long Turn + Discussion**

- Set a timer for **2 minutes** and deliver a solo long turn on this prompt:
  *"Compare these two images showing different approaches to urban development. What challenges and opportunities does each approach present for local communities?"*
- Record yourself on your phone or laptop — **do not stop or restart**.
- After recording, listen back and evaluate using these three C1 criteria:
  - ✅ Did you use a **range of complex structures** (conditionals, passive constructions, nominalisations)?
  - ✅ Did you **avoid long pauses** or filler words like "erm" and "you know"?
  - ✅ Did you **develop your ideas** rather than just describe?
- Then respond to this follow-up discussion question for **3 minutes** (also recorded):
  *"To what extent should governments prioritise sustainable urban planning over economic growth?"*

### 📅 Wednesday — Writing Masterclass: Mock Task + Deep Revision

**Daily Focus:** Producing a full C1 writing task under timed conditions, then conducting a rigorous self-edit.

---

#### ✍️ Writing — *90 minutes total*

**Activity: Full Mock Writing Task (Part 1 + Part 2)**

**Part 1 — Essay (45 minutes):**
Write a **formal discursive essay** (220–260 words) in response to this prompt:
> *"Technological advancement is the most significant driver of social change in the 21st century." Discuss, using the two points below and adding a point of your own.*
> - *The role of social media in shaping public opinion*
> - *Automation and its impact on employment*

**Part 2 — Report (45 minutes):**
Write a **formal report** (220–260 words) for this task:
> *You have been asked by your college principal to write a report on the effectiveness of the current English language support services available to international students, and to make recommendations for improvement.*

**Self-Editing Protocol (built into the 90 minutes — last 10 minutes per task):**
After writing each task, review specifically for:
- 🔍 **Lexical range** — Have you used at least 8–10 C1-level vocabulary items? (e.g. *proliferation, contentious, mitigate, underscore*)
- 🔍 **Cohesion** — Are your paragraphs linked with sophisticated discourse markers? (*Notwithstanding this... / A further consideration is... / It stands to reason that...*)
- 🔍 **Register consistency** — Is every sentence appropriately formal?

## 💡 Three C1-Specific Tips for Week 7

---

### 🔑 Tip 1: Treat Every Mock Task as the Real Exam — No Exceptions

This week, the **conditions matter as much as the content**. Sit at a desk. Remove distractions. Use a timer. Don't pause audio. Don't re-read questions after time is up. The psychological habit of performing under pressure is a **trainable skill**, and Week 7 is your last real opportunity to train it before the final polish of Week 8. Candidates who underperform in exams often know the material — they simply haven't practised *performing* under exam conditions enough.

---

### 🔑 Tip 2: Error Analysis Is Not Self-Criticism — It's Intelligence Gathering

Many students feel deflated when they make mistakes in mock tasks. **Reframe this completely.** Every error you identify this week is an error that will *not* surprise you on exam day. The goal of your Error Analysis Notebook is not to catalogue your failures — it is to build a **personalised exam strategy**. A student who knows their three recurring error types and has a plan to manage them is far better prepared than a student who scored higher on mocks but never examined *why* they got things wrong.

---

### 🔑 Tip 3: Upgrade Your Spoken and Written Register with One Powerful Technique — Nominalisation

One of the clearest markers of C1 proficiency is the ability to **nominalise** — to express ideas using noun phrases rather than simple verb clauses. Compare:

- ❌ *"The government decided to invest in renewable energy, which was very important."*
- ✅ *"The government's decision to invest in renewable energy represented a significant policy shift."*

The second sentence is denser, more formal, and more precise — exactly what examiners reward at C1. This week, every time you write or speak, **consciously convert at least one verb-based clause into a noun phrase**. Over time, this becomes automatic and dramatically elevates your language level.
