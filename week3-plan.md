# 📚 C1 English Certificate Exam — Weekly Study Plan
## Week 3: Deepening Comprehension

---

### 🎯 Weekly Theme & Learning Objectives

This week, you move beyond surface-level understanding and begin working with **depth, complexity, and synthesis**. You'll develop strategies for navigating multiple texts simultaneously, sustain longer, more structured spoken monologues, and practise the art of summarisation — a core mediation skill at C1 level. By Friday, you should feel noticeably more confident handling dense, authentic English content without losing your thread.

---

## 📅 Day-by-Day Breakdown

---

### 🗓️ MONDAY — *Reading as a Strategic Act*

**Daily Focus:** Multiple-text reading strategies and inference skills

---

#### 📖 READING — *55 minutes*
**Activity: Comparative Text Analysis**
- Find **two articles on the same topic** (e.g., artificial intelligence, climate policy, or remote work) from **The Guardian** (theguardian.com) and **BBC News** (bbc.co.uk/news).
- Read both articles using the **SQ3R method**: Survey headings → form Questions → Read actively → Recite key points → Review.
- After reading, create a **two-column comparison table** noting: main argument, tone, evidence used, and implied audience.
- Identify **at least 5 inferential points** — things the author implies but does not state directly.

> 🔗 **Resources:**
> - The Guardian: theguardian.com/environment or /technology
> - BBC News Analysis section: bbc.co.uk/news/explainers

---

#### 🗣️ SPEAKING — *30 minutes*
**Activity: Responding to What You Read**
- Choose **one strong opinion** from either article and record yourself giving a **2-minute spoken response**.
- Focus on: introducing your stance clearly, supporting it with evidence from the text, and ending with a forward-looking comment.
- Play it back and note: Did you hesitate? Did you use discourse markers (*Furthermore, In contrast, What strikes me most is...*)? Did you finish your sentences?

> 🔗 **Resource:** BBC Learning English – "Talk About" series: bbc.co.uk/learningenglish

---

#### 👂 LISTENING — *30 minutes*
**Activity: Gist + Detail Listening**
- Watch a **TED Talk of 10–15 minutes** on a topic related to your reading (search "TED Talk AI" or "TED Talk climate").
- First listen: **no subtitles** — note only the main argument and 3 supporting ideas.
- Second listen: **with subtitles** — check your notes and add detail. Underline any vocabulary you didn't catch the first time.

> 🔗 **Resource:** TED Talks: ted.com/talks — filter by topic and duration

---

#### ✍️ WRITING — *20 minutes*
**Activity: Vocabulary Harvesting**
- From your two articles and TED Talk, collect **10 high-frequency C1 collocations or phrases** (e.g., *mounting evidence, far-reaching consequences, a nuanced approach*).
- Write **one original sentence** for each, using a different topic from the one you found it in. This forces genuine understanding rather than copying.

---

#### 🔄 MEDIATION — *25 minutes*
**Activity: Introduction to Written Summarisation**
- Take **one of your two articles** and write a **150-word neutral summary** as if explaining it to someone who hasn't read it.
- Rules: No direct quotes. No personal opinion. Use your own sentence structures. Preserve the author's key message accurately.
- This is a foundational mediation skill — you are acting as a **bridge between the text and the reader**.

### 🗓️ TUESDAY — *Listening with Precision*

**Daily Focus:** Extended listening, note-taking under pressure, and spoken vocabulary in context

---

#### 👂 LISTENING — *55 minutes*
**Activity: Structured Note-Taking from a Podcast**
- Listen to a **full episode (20–30 minutes)** of a high-quality English podcast such as *In Our Time* (BBC Radio 4) or *The Documentary* (BBC World Service).
- Use the **Cornell Note-Taking System**: divide your page into a narrow left column (keywords/questions) and a wide right column (notes). Leave space at the bottom for a summary.
- After listening, write a **5-sentence summary** using only your notes — not the audio again.

> 🔗 **Resources:**
> - BBC In Our Time: bbc.co.uk/programmes/b006qykl/episodes/downloads
> - BBC World Service Documentaries: bbc.co.uk/programmes/p02nq0lx/episodes/downloads

---

#### 🗣️ SPEAKING — *40 minutes*
**Activity: Extended Monologue Practice — 3 Minutes**
- Choose a **complex topic** (e.g., *"Is social media doing more harm than good to public discourse?"*).
- Prepare for **5 minutes only** — bullet points, not a script.
- Record a **3-minute spoken monologue**. Structure it as: **Position → Argument 1 with example → Argument 2 with example → Counterargument acknowledged → Conclusion**.
- Evaluate yourself: Did you reach 3 minutes? Did you maintain coherence throughout? Were your examples specific?

> 🔗 **Resource:** British Council – Speaking skills: britishcouncil.org/english/learn-online

---

#### 📖 READING — *30 minutes*
**Activity: Skimming and Scanning Under Time Pressure**
- Find a **long-form article** (1,000+ words) from *The Economist* (economist.com — free articles available) or *The Atlantic*.
- Set a timer for **8 minutes** and skim the article. Then answer these questions **from memory**:
  1. What is the central argument?
  2. What evidence is provided?
  3. What is the author's tone?
  4. What conclusion is drawn?
- This simulates C1 exam reading conditions where time management is critical.

---

#### ✍️ WRITING — *35 minutes*
**Activity: Paragraph Development — The PEEL Method**
- Write **two fully developed paragraphs** on the topic from your speaking activity today.
- Use the **PEEL structure**: **P**oint → **E**vidence → **E**xplanation → **L**ink back.
- Each paragraph should be **100–120 words**. Focus on: precise vocabulary, varied sentence length, and logical flow between sentences.

---

#### 🔄 MEDIATION — *20 minutes*
**Activity: Oral Summarisation**
- Listen to the first **5 minutes** of your podcast again.
- Record yourself giving a **90-second spoken summary** of those 5 minutes — as if briefing a colleague who missed it.
- Focus on: neutral tone, accurate representation, and clear sequencing (*First, the speaker argues... This is supported by... Finally...*).

### 🗓️ WEDNESDAY — *The Art of Speaking at Length*

**Daily Focus:** Monologue fluency, discourse organisation, and reading for argument structure

---

#### 🗣️ SPEAKING — *55 minutes*
**Activity: Structured Long-Turn Practice**
- This is your **dedicated speaking development day**.
- Choose **three different topics** from this list:
  1. *The role of technology in modern education*
  2. *Whether cities should ban private cars*
  3. *The value of learning a second language*
- For each topic, speak for **90 seconds without stopping**. Record all three attempts.
- After each recording, score yourself on:
  - ✅ Fluency (no long pauses)
  - ✅ Coherence (logical progression)
  - ✅ Lexical range (varied vocabulary, no repetition)
  - ✅ Grammar accuracy (complex structures attempted)

> 🔗 **Resource:** Cambridge English – Speaking exam overview and sample tasks:
> cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/advanced/preparation/

---

#### 👂 LISTENING — *30 minutes*
**Activity: Identifying Speaker Attitude and Implicit Meaning**
- Watch a **debate or panel discussion** on YouTube (search "Intelligence Squared debate" or "BBC Question Time clip").
- Focus not on *what* is said but *how* it is said. Note:
  - Hedging language (*It could be argued, One might suggest...*)
  - Emphasis and stress patterns
  - Moments where the speaker implies disagreement without stating it
- Write **5 observations** about how speakers signal attitude implicitly.

> 🔗 **Resource:** Intelligence Squared Debates: intelligencesquared.com/videos

---

#### 📖 READING — *35 minutes*
**Activity: Argument Mapping**
- Read a **Cambridge English sample reading text** or a long opinion piece from *The Guardian's Comment is Free* section.
- Create an **argument map**: a visual diagram showing the central claim, supporting arguments, counterarguments, and the author's responses to those counterarguments.
- This technique trains you to see the **architecture of a text**, not just its content — essential for C1 reading tasks.

> 🔗 **Resource:** Cambridge English sample materials: cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/advanced/preparation/

---

#### ✍️ WRITING — *30 minutes*
**Activity: Discourse Marker Upgrade**
- Take a paragraph you wrote earlier this week and **rewrite it twice**:
  - Version 1: Using **addition and contrast markers** (*Moreover, Nevertheless, By contrast, In addition to this...*)
  - Version 2: Using **cause and result markers** (*As a consequence, This inevitably leads to, Given that...*)
- Compare all three versions. Which reads most naturally at C1 level? Why?

---

#### 🔄 MEDIATION — *30 minutes*
**Activity: Cross-Text Mediation**
- Read **two short opinion pieces** (300–400 words each) with opposing views on the same topic.
- Write a **200-word mediation text** that presents **both perspectives fairly** without taking sides.
- This is a direct simulation of C1 mediation exam tasks. Use phrases like: *Proponents argue that... On the other hand, critics contend... Both sides agree, however, that...*

### 🗓️ THURSDAY — *Writing with Precision and Purpose*

**Daily Focus:** Summarisation writing, complex sentence construction, and reading for vocabulary

---

#### ✍️ WRITING — *55 minutes*
**Activity: Formal Summary Writing**
- Find a **400–500 word article** on a social or environmental issue.
- Write a **formal summary of 180–200 words** following these rules:
  1. Open with the article's main claim (paraphrased, not quoted)
  2. Present 2–3 supporting points in logical order
  3. Note any significant counterargument the author addresses
  4. Close with the author's conclusion
- Then **self-edit** for 10 minutes: check for passive voice where appropriate, nominalisation (*the development of* rather than *developing*), and impersonal constructions (*It is widely acknowledged that...*).

> 🔗 **Resource:** British Council – Writing C1 tasks: learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/skills/writing

---

#### 📖 READING — *40 minutes*
**Activity: Vocabulary in Context — Deep Reading**
- Choose a **dense academic or journalistic text** (try *Aeon Magazine*: aeon.co — free, intellectually rich articles).
- Read slowly and deliberately. For every paragraph:
  - Identify the **topic sentence**
  - Note **one word or phrase** you wouldn't have used yourself
  - Consider: *Could I use this in my writing or speaking?*
- Build a **personal C1 vocabulary log** — a running document you add to every day this week.

> 🔗 **Resource:** Aeon Magazine: aeon.co (free, no subscription needed)

---

#### 👂 LISTENING — *35 minutes*
**Activity: Listening for Text Organisation**
- Listen to a **BBC Radio 4 documentary or feature** (15–20 minutes).
- This time, focus on **how the content is organised**: Does it follow a problem-solution structure? A chronological narrative? A compare-contrast format?
- Draw a **simple flowchart** of the programme's structure. This skill transfers directly to your reading and writing.

> 🔗 **Resource:** BBC Radio 4 – Available episodes: bbc.co.uk/radio4

---

#### 🗣️ SPEAKING — *25 minutes*
**Activity: Paraphrasing and Reformulation**
- Take **5 sentences** from your reading today and practise saying each one in **two different ways** — without changing the meaning.
- Example: *"The policy has faced significant opposition"* → *"There has been considerable resistance to this measure"* → *"Many have pushed back strongly against this approach."*
- This builds the **lexical flexibility** that C1 examiners reward.

---

#### 🔄 MEDIATION — *25 minutes*
**Activity: Note-Assisted Oral Mediation**
- Read a **300-word text** on a topic of your choice.
- Make brief notes (maximum 8 bullet points).
- Put the text away. Using only your notes, **record a 2-minute spoken explanation** of the text's content for an imaginary listener who doesn't speak English well enough to read it themselves.
- This is the essence of mediation: **transferring meaning across a communication gap**.

## 💡 Three C1-Specific Tips for Week 3

---

### Tip 1: 🧠 Read the *Architecture*, Not Just the Content
At C1 level, examiners test whether you understand **how** a text works, not just **what** it says. When you read this week, always ask: *Why did the author put this paragraph here? What function does this sentence serve?* Recognising rhetorical moves — concession, exemplification, refutation — will transform your reading comprehension scores and make your own writing far more sophisticated.

---

### Tip 2: 🎙️ Silence Is Not Your Enemy — But Filler Words Are
When practising your monologues, you may feel the urge to fill pauses with *"um," "like," or "you know."* At C1 level, a **brief, purposeful pause** sounds far more confident and fluent than a stream of filler words. Train yourself to replace fillers with **thinking phrases**: *"That's an interesting point to consider..."* / *"What I find particularly significant here is..."* These buy you thinking time while simultaneously demonstrating C1-level discourse management.

---

### Tip 3: ✍️ Nominalisation Is Your Writing Superpower
One of the clearest markers of C1 writing is the use of **nominalisation** — turning verbs and adjectives into nouns to create a more formal, academic register. Compare:
- ❌ *"The government decided to change the policy, which affected many people."*
- ✅ *"The government's decision to reform the policy had far-reaching implications for a significant proportion of the population."*

This week, every time you write a sentence with a simple verb, challenge yourself to **nominalise it**. It will feel unnatural at first — that discomfort means you're growing. 💪
