## 🎯 Weekly Theme & Learning Objectives

This week marks your **mid-program checkpoint** — a powerful opportunity to consolidate everything you've built over the past three weeks across all five skills. Rather than introducing entirely new content, you'll be working with **mixed-skill, timed practice tasks** that mirror real exam conditions, helping you identify both your strengths and the areas that need the most attention in the weeks ahead.

By the end of Week 4, you will be able to:
- Perform confidently under timed conditions across reading, writing, listening, speaking, and mediation tasks
- Accurately self-assess your current C1 level using structured reflection tools
- Identify specific language gaps (vocabulary, grammar, discourse) and create a targeted action plan for Weeks 5–8

## 📅 Day-by-Day Breakdown

---

### 🗓️ MONDAY — *"Timed Reading & Reflective Writing"*
**Focus: Reading + Writing | Theme: Society & Change**

---

#### 📖 READING — *Multiple-Choice & Text Structure*
**⏱️ 45 minutes**

- **Activity:** Find a long-form article (800–1,000 words) from **The Guardian's Society section** (theguardian.com/society) on a topic such as urban inequality, digital culture, or generational change.
- **Task:** Read the article **once quickly** (4 minutes) for gist, then **once carefully** (10 minutes) for detail. Afterwards, without looking at the text, write down:
  - The main argument
  - Three supporting points
  - One counterargument the author acknowledges
- **Then:** Return to the text and check your notes. Highlight any **discourse markers** (e.g., *nevertheless, in contrast, it stands to reason that*) and add them to your vocabulary log.
- **C1 Skill Focus:** Identifying implicit meaning, authorial stance, and text organisation — all tested in the C1 reading exam.

**🔗 Resource:** [The Guardian – Society](https://www.theguardian.com/society)

---

#### ✍️ WRITING — *Timed Opinion Essay (First Draft)*
**⏱️ 50 minutes**

- **Activity:** Write a **timed opinion essay** (220–260 words) in response to this prompt:

  > *"The rapid pace of technological change benefits society as a whole. To what extent do you agree?"*

- **Rules:** Set a timer. Do **not** use a dictionary or grammar checker during writing. This simulates real exam conditions.
- **Structure to follow:**
  - **Introduction** (2–3 sentences): Paraphrase the question + state your position
  - **Body Paragraph 1** (supporting your view with evidence/example)
  - **Body Paragraph 2** (acknowledging the opposing view + refuting it)
  - **Conclusion** (2–3 sentences): Restate position + broader implication
- **After writing:** Use the **Cambridge C1 Writing Assessment Criteria** (Content, Communicative Achievement, Organisation, Language) to give yourself a score out of 5 for each category. Be honest.

**🔗 Resource:** [Cambridge English – Writing Guides](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/learning-english/activities-for-learners/?skill=writing)

---

### 🗓️ TUESDAY — *"Extended Listening & Spoken Response"*
**Focus: Listening + Speaking | Theme: Ideas & Innovation**

---

#### 🎧 LISTENING — *Lecture-Style Comprehension*
**⏱️ 40 minutes**

- **Activity:** Go to **TED Talks** and choose a talk of **12–18 minutes** on a topic related to innovation, psychology, or society (avoid talks you've seen before). Suggested options:
  - *"The puzzle of motivation"* — Dan Pink
  - *"How great leaders inspire action"* — Simon Sinek
  - *"The surprising science of happiness"* — Dan Gilbert

- **Task — Three-Stage Listening:**
  1. **First listen (no notes):** What is the speaker's central message? Write one sentence.
  2. **Second listen (with notes):** Note down key arguments, examples, and any surprising statistics.
  3. **Third listen (targeted):** Focus only on the speaker's **rhetorical techniques** — how do they use repetition, contrast, or rhetorical questions to persuade?

- **Vocabulary task:** Write down **5 phrases** the speaker uses that you could incorporate into your own speaking or writing.

**🔗 Resource:** [TED Talks](https://www.ted.com/talks)

---

#### 🗣️ SPEAKING — *Structured Monologue Response*
**⏱️ 35 minutes**

- **Activity:** Based on the TED Talk you just listened to, prepare and deliver a **2-minute spoken response** (record yourself on your phone or laptop).

- **Your response should include:**
  - A brief summary of the speaker's main point (30 seconds)
  - Your personal reaction — do you agree, disagree, or partially agree? (60 seconds)
  - A real-world example or personal connection to the topic (30 seconds)

- **After recording:** Play it back and evaluate yourself on:
  - ✅ Did you speak for the full 2 minutes without stopping?
  - ✅ Did you use **discourse markers** to organise your ideas? (*firstly, what struck me most was, on the other hand...*)
  - ✅ Did you avoid repeating the same vocabulary more than twice?
  - ✅ Was your pronunciation clear and your pace controlled?

- **Write down one specific thing to improve** and keep it visible for the rest of the week.

**🔗 Resource:** [BBC Learning English – Pronunciation & Fluency](https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/english/features/pronunciation)

---

### 🗓️ WEDNESDAY — *"Mediation Marathon & Vocabulary Audit"*
**Focus: Mediation + Vocabulary Consolidation | Theme: Environment & Responsibility**

---

#### 🔄 MEDIATION — *Cross-Text Summary for a Specific Audience*
**⏱️ 50 minutes**

- **Activity:** Read **two short texts** (you'll find these easily by searching The Guardian or BBC News):
  - **Text A:** A scientific or academic article about climate change impacts (aim for something data-heavy)
  - **Text B:** A personal opinion piece or blog post from someone sceptical of climate policy

- **Task:** Imagine you are preparing a **briefing note** for a group of secondary school students (aged 15–16) who have no background in the topic. Write **180–220 words** that:
  - Fairly represent **both perspectives**
  - Use **neutral, accessible language** (no jargon without explanation)
  - Highlight **one point of agreement** and **one key difference** between the two texts
  - End with a **thought-provoking question** for the students to consider

- **C1 Mediation Skill:** This task tests your ability to transform and relay information for a new audience — a core C1 competency. Focus on **reformulation**, not copying.

**🔗 Resources:**
- [BBC News – Environment](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/science-environment)
- [The Guardian – Environment](https://www.theguardian.com/environment)

---

#### 📝 VOCABULARY AUDIT — *Weeks 1–3 Review*
**⏱️ 30 minutes**

- **Activity:** Go back through your vocabulary log from Weeks 1, 2, and 3.
- **Task:**
  1. Identify **10 words or phrases** you haven't used in speaking or writing yet
  2. For each one, write a **single original sentence** that demonstrates its meaning in context
  3. Mark any words you still feel uncertain about with a ⭐ — these become your **priority vocabulary** for Weeks 5–8
  4. Test yourself: cover the definitions and see how many you can recall from the word alone

- **Bonus challenge:** Can you use **3 of your priority words** naturally in tomorrow's speaking task?

**🔗 Resource:** [Quizlet](https://quizlet.com) — create a digital flashcard set for your C1 vocabulary

---

### 🗓️ THURSDAY — *"Grammar Under Pressure & Listening Detail"*
**Focus: Grammar Consolidation + Listening | Theme: Work & Identity*

---

#### 🎧 LISTENING — *Detail & Inference Practice*
**⏱️ 35 minutes**

- **Activity:** Visit **BBC Learning English – 6 Minute English** and choose **two episodes** from the archive on different topics (each is approximately 6 minutes).

- **Task for each episode:**
  - Listen **once** and answer: What is the main topic? What is one opinion expressed?
  - Listen **again** and note: What **specific vocabulary** do the presenters use that is C1-level?
  - Listen a **third time** and focus on **intonation and stress** — which words do the speakers emphasise, and why?

- **Reflection question:** In the C1 listening exam, you'll often need to identify a speaker's **attitude or feeling**, not just the facts. Practise asking yourself: *"How does this speaker feel about what they're saying?"*

**🔗 Resource:** [BBC Learning English – 6 Minute English](https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/english/features/6-minute-english)

---

#### 📐 GRAMMAR CONSOLIDATION — *Timed Error Correction*
**⏱️ 40 minutes**

- **Activity:** This is a **self-audit grammar sprint**. Take your written work from Monday's essay and Tuesday's speaking notes and do the following:

  **Part 1 — Error Hunt (15 minutes):**
  Read through your Monday essay and identify any errors in:
  - Subject-verb agreement
  - Article use (a/an/the/zero article)
  - Tense consistency
  - Passive voice construction
  - Conditional structures (mixed conditionals are a C1 marker)

  **Part 2 — Upgrade Challenge (15 minutes):**
  Choose **5 sentences** from your essay that feel "safe" or simple. Rewrite each one using a more complex C1 structure, such as:
  - Inversion (*"Not only does technology improve efficiency, but it also..."*)
  - Cleft sentences (*"It is the speed of change that concerns many experts..."*)
  - Nominalization (*"The rapid development of AI has led to..."*)

  **Part 3 — Quick Reference (10 minutes):**
  Visit the **British Council Grammar Reference** and review **one grammar point** you struggled with this week.

**🔗 Resource:** [British Council – Grammar](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/grammar)

### 🗓️ FRIDAY — *"Full Mixed-Skills Simulation Day"*
**Focus: All Five Skills | Theme: Free Choice**

> *Friday is your mini exam day. Choose a topic you find genuinely interesting — this will make the simulation feel more natural and less stressful.*

---

#### 🔄 FULL SIMULATION SEQUENCE
**⏱️ Total: 3 hours (with short breaks)**

Complete the following tasks **in order**, with a **5-minute break between each**:

---

**Task 1 — Reading (40 minutes)**
- Find a **long-form article** (900+ words) from **The Economist** or **The Atlantic** on your chosen topic.
- Read it under timed conditions and answer these questions **in writing**:
  1. What is the writer's main argument? (2–3 sentences)
  2. Identify two pieces of evidence the writer uses. Are they convincing?
  3. What does the phrase *[choose a complex phrase from the text]* mean in context?
  4. What is the writer's tone — objective, critical, optimistic? Justify your answer with a quote.

**🔗 Resources:**
- [The Economist](https://www.economist.com) (some free articles available)
- [The Atlantic](https://www.theatlantic.com)

---

**Task 2 — Listening (30 minutes)**
- Find a **podcast episode or documentary clip** (10–15 minutes) related to your chosen topic on **YouTube** or **BBC Sounds**.
- Take structured notes using this format:
  - **Main topic:**
  - **Key point 1 + supporting detail:**
  - **Key point 2 + supporting detail:**
  - **Speaker's attitude/tone:**
  - **One thing I didn't fully understand:**

**🔗 Resource:** [BBC Sounds](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sounds)

---

**Task 3 — Mediation (25 minutes)**
- Using your reading article and listening notes, write a **150-word summary** for a friend who has no knowledge of the topic. Your summary must:
  - Be written in your own words (no copying)
  - Include the main idea from **both** sources
  - Use **linking language** to connect ideas smoothly

---

**Task 4 — Speaking (20 minutes)**
- Record a **3-minute spoken response** to this question about your chosen topic:

  > *"What do you think is the most important challenge or opportunity in this area, and why?"*

- Aim for: clear structure, varied vocabulary, natural fluency, and at least **one hedging phrase** (*"It could be argued that...", "There's a strong case for..."*)

---

**Task 5 — Writing (45 minutes)**
- Write a **formal letter or report** (220–260 words) related to your topic. For example:
  - A letter to a newspaper editor responding to the article you read
  - A short report summarising findings for a fictional organisation

- Focus on: **register consistency**, **formal vocabulary**, and **clear paragraph structure**

---

**End of Day Reflection (10 minutes):**
Write 3–5 sentences answering: *"Which skill felt strongest today, and which felt most challenging? What will I prioritise in Week 5?"*

## 🌅 Weekend Tasks

### 📌 Weekend Task 1 — *Error Analysis & Rewriting (Saturday)*
**⏱️ 60 minutes**

- Gather **all written work** from this week (Monday essay, Wednesday mediation, Friday writing task).
- Read through everything with fresh eyes and create an **Error Log** with three columns:

| Error Type | Original Sentence | Corrected Version |
|---|---|---|
| e.g. Wrong tense | *"By 2030, technology will changed..."* | *"By 2030, technology will have changed..."* |

- Aim to identify at least **10 errors** across all pieces.
- For each error, ask yourself: *"Is this a careless mistake or a knowledge gap?"* — this distinction matters for your Week 5 planning.
- Rewrite your **Monday essay** incorporating all corrections and upgrades. This final version should feel noticeably stronger than your first draft.

---

### 📌 Weekend Task 2 — *Extensive Reading for Pleasure (Sunday)*
**⏱️ 45–60 minutes**

- Choose something you **genuinely want to read** — a long article, a chapter of a novel, a quality magazine feature. The only rule: it must be in English and at or above your current level.
- Suggested sources:
  - **Delayed Gratification magazine** (slow journalism, beautifully written)
  - **Aeon Essays** (aeon.co — long-form ideas journalism, free)
  - A chapter from any contemporary literary fiction novel

- **Task:** As you read, simply **underline or highlight** any phrases that strike you as particularly well-written, surprising, or useful. Don't stop to look things up — let yourself enjoy the experience of reading.
- Afterwards, spend **10 minutes** adding your favourite 5 phrases to your vocabulary log.

> *Sunday's task is a reminder that language learning isn't only about exam preparation — it's about building a genuine relationship with English. The more you enjoy it, the faster you'll improve.*

**🔗 Resource:** [Aeon Essays](https://aeon.co/essays)

## 💡 Three C1-Specific Tips for Week 4

---

### Tip 1: 🎯 *Timed Practice Is Non-Negotiable at This Stage*

By Week 4, you should be moving away from "comfortable" practice and into **exam-realistic conditions**. The C1 exam is not just a test of what you know — it's a test of what you can produce **under pressure and within time limits**. Every time you write an essay without a timer or listen to something you can pause and rewind indefinitely, you're practising a skill that won't exist on exam day. This week, commit to at least **three timed tasks** and resist the urge to give yourself extra time. Discomfort now means confidence later.

---

### Tip 2: 📊 *Self-Assessment Is a C1 Skill in Itself*

One of the hallmarks of a C1 learner is **metacognitive awareness** — the ability to reflect on your own language use and identify what works and what doesn't. Don't treat the self-assessment checkpoint as a box-ticking exercise. The students who improve fastest are those who can look at their own writing and say: *"This sentence is grammatically correct, but it's not C1 — it's too simple. How can I restructure it?"* Develop this critical inner voice this week, and it will serve you in every task from here to exam day.

---

### Tip 3: 🔗 *Connect Your Skills — They're Not Separate Subjects*

A common mistake at this stage is treating Reading, Writing, Listening, Speaking, and Mediation as five completely separate subjects. At C1 level, the most powerful practice is **cross-skill integration** — using what you read as material for speaking, using what you hear as inspiration for writing, using written texts as the basis for mediation. Friday's simulation day is designed exactly for this. Notice how your **vocabulary naturally transfers** between skills when you work on a single topic across multiple tasks. This is how fluency is built.
