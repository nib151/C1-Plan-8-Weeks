# 🎯 C1 English Certificate Exam — Weekly Study Plan
## Week 1: Foundations & Diagnostic — *"Know Where You Stand"*

---

### 📌 Weekly Theme & Learning Objectives

Welcome to the beginning of your C1 journey! This week is all about **honest self-assessment and awareness**. You'll explore what C1-level performance actually looks and sounds like across all five skills — Speaking, Listening, Mediation, Writing, and Reading — so you can identify your strengths and target your weak spots with precision. By Friday, you won't just know *what* C1 requires; you'll have a clear, personalised map of where you're starting from.

## 📅 Day-by-Day Breakdown

### 🗓️ Monday — *"What Does C1 Sound Like?"*
**Theme: Speaking & Listening Diagnostic**

---

#### 🗣️ Speaking — *45 minutes*
**Activity: C1 Speaking Benchmark Listening + Self-Recording**

- Go to the **Cambridge English YouTube channel** and watch 2–3 sample C1 Advanced (CAE) Speaking test videos. Pay close attention to how candidates:
  - Develop ideas at length without being prompted
  - Use discourse markers (*"What's particularly interesting here is...", "Building on that point..."*)
  - Handle hesitation naturally without losing fluency
- After watching, **record yourself for 2 minutes** answering this prompt:
  > *"Describe a time when you had to adapt to a significant change. What did you learn from it?"*
- Listen back critically. Ask yourself:
  - Did I speak for the full 2 minutes without major pauses?
  - Did I use a range of vocabulary or repeat the same words?
  - Did my ideas connect logically?
- **Write 3 honest observations** about your spoken performance in a dedicated study notebook.

**🔗 Resource:** [Cambridge English Official YouTube Channel](https://www.youtube.com/user/cambridgeenglishtv) — search *"C1 Advanced Speaking test"*

---

#### 👂 Listening — *40 minutes*
**Activity: C1 Listening Diagnostic Task**

- Visit the **British Council's LearnEnglish website** and complete one of their **C1-level listening exercises** (look under "Skills" → "Listening" → filter by C1).
- Then, listen to one episode of **BBC Radio 4's "In Our Time"** (any topic that interests you — science, history, philosophy). Listen for **10 minutes only**.
- While listening, note:
  - How many words or phrases you didn't understand
  - Whether you could follow the *main argument* even without catching every word
  - Any moments where the speed or accent caused difficulty
- **Don't look up every word yet** — this is a diagnostic, not a study session. You're measuring your current tolerance for authentic C1 listening.

**🔗 Resources:**
- [British Council LearnEnglish Listening](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/skills/listening)
- [BBC Radio 4 In Our Time](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b006qykl/episodes/downloads)

#### 🌉 Mediation — *30 minutes*
**Activity: Introduction to Mediation — What Is It?**

Mediation is often the **least familiar skill** for C1 candidates, so today is purely about understanding what it involves.

- Read the **Cambridge English guide to mediation** (search: *"Cambridge C1 Advanced mediation task explained"*) or watch a short explainer video on YouTube.
- Mediation at C1 means: **conveying information from one source to another person**, adapting register, summarising key points, and adding your own evaluative comments.
- **Mini-task:** Read any short news article from **The Guardian** (3–4 paragraphs). Then, without looking at it, explain the main points **aloud or in writing** as if you were telling a friend who doesn't speak English well. Keep it natural and in your own words.
- Reflect: Was it easy to paraphrase? Did you find yourself translating mentally?

**🔗 Resources:**
- [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com) — any current affairs article
- Search YouTube: *"C1 Advanced mediation task explained"*

#### ✍️ Writing — *45 minutes*
**Activity: Diagnostic Essay — Unpolished First Draft**

Write a **short, timed piece** (250–300 words) in **30 minutes** on the following prompt:
> *"Technology has made modern life easier but less meaningful. To what extent do you agree?"*

**Rules for this diagnostic:**
- ⏱️ Set a timer. Do NOT go over 30 minutes.
- Do NOT use a dictionary or grammar checker.
- Write whatever comes naturally — this is your baseline.

After writing, spend the remaining 15 minutes evaluating your own work using these C1 criteria:
- ✅ Did I have a clear introduction, body paragraphs, and conclusion?
- ✅ Did I use any complex sentence structures (relative clauses, conditionals, passive voice)?
- ✅ Did I express a nuanced opinion or just agree/disagree simply?
- ✅ Did I use formal, academic vocabulary or mostly everyday words?

**Save this essay.** You'll compare it to your Week 8 writing to see your progress.

**🔗 Resource:** [Cambridge C1 Writing Assessment Criteria](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/advanced/preparation/) — download the sample papers

---

#### 📖 Reading — *40 minutes*
**Activity: C1 Reading Speed & Comprehension Diagnostic**

- Download or access a **free C1 Advanced sample Reading paper** from the Cambridge English website (Part 1 is a good starting point — multiple choice with a long text).
- Complete **one reading part only** under timed conditions (approximately 18–20 minutes for one part).
- Check your answers using the answer key.
- For every question you got wrong, go back to the text and find **exactly where the correct answer is**. This is called *evidence-based reading* and it's a core C1 skill.

**🔗 Resource:** [Cambridge C1 Advanced Free Sample Papers](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/advanced/preparation/) — click "Preparation materials"

### 🗓️ Tuesday — *"Reading Between the Lines"*
**Theme: Reading Strategies & Vocabulary Awareness**

---

#### 🗣️ Speaking — *30 minutes*
**Activity: Vocabulary in Context — Speaking Practice**

- Choose **5 advanced words or phrases** you encountered in Monday's reading text (e.g., *"contentious," "shed light on," "far-reaching implications"*).
- For each word, **say two sentences aloud** using it in a different context from where you found it.
- Then answer this question aloud for 90 seconds:
  > *"What role does reading play in your life, and has it changed as you've grown older?"*
- Focus on **incorporating at least 2 of your new words** naturally into your answer.

---

#### 👂 Listening — *35 minutes*
**Activity: TED Talk — Focused Listening for Main Ideas**

- Watch a **TED Talk** of your choice (aim for 12–15 minutes, with a clear argument structure). Recommended for this week:
  > *"The Power of Vulnerability" — Brené Brown* or *"Do Schools Kill Creativity?" — Ken Robinson*
- **First listen:** No subtitles. Just listen for the **main argument and 3 supporting points**.
- **Second listen (same section, 5 minutes only):** Turn on English subtitles. Notice any words or phrases you missed.
- Write a **5-sentence summary** of the talk's core message.

**🔗 Resource:** [TED Talks](https://www.ted.com) — filter by "Most Popular"

---

#### 🌉 Mediation — *30 minutes*
**Activity: Written Mediation — Summarise for a Specific Audience**

- Find a short article on **BBC News** about a current social or environmental issue (2–3 paragraphs).
- Write a **100-word summary** of the article as if you were explaining it to:
  - A) A teenager with no background knowledge
  - B) A professional colleague in a formal email
- Notice how your **vocabulary, tone, and sentence structure** change between the two versions. This audience-awareness is central to C1 mediation.

**🔗 Resource:** [BBC News](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news)

---

#### ✍️ Writing — *35 minutes*
**Activity: Sentence-Level Grammar Awareness**

This is not a full writing task — it's a **grammar diagnostic**.

- Go to **BBC Learning English's "Grammar" section** and take their **Advanced Grammar Quiz** or complete exercises tagged as C1/Advanced.
- Focus particularly on:
  - Inversion (*"Not only did she...", "Rarely have I..."*)
  - Mixed conditionals (*"If I had studied harder, I would be..."*)
  - Cleft sentences (*"It was the lack of preparation that caused..."*)
- For each structure you find difficult, write **2 original sentences** using it. Don't copy examples — create your own.

**🔗 Resource:** [BBC Learning English Grammar](https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/english/features/grammar)

---

#### 📖 Reading — *45 minutes*
**Activity: Skimming, Scanning & Inference Practice**

C1 reading requires three distinct sub-skills. Today you'll practise all three.

- Choose a **long-form article** from **The Guardian's "Long Reads" section** (aim for 800–1,000 words).
- **Skim** (3 minutes): Read only the first sentence of each paragraph. What is the article about?
- **Scan** (5 minutes): Find answers to 3 questions you write yourself before reading in detail.
- **Infer** (15 minutes): Read the full article. Identify **2 things the author implies but never directly states**. Write them down with evidence from the text.

**🔗 Resource:** [The Guardian Long Reads](https://www.theguardian.com/news/series/the-long-read)

### 🗓️ Wednesday — *"Finding Your Voice"*
**Theme: Speaking Fluency & Writing Structure**

---

#### 🗣️ Speaking — *45 minutes*
**Activity: Structured Monologue Practice**

At C1, you need to speak **at length, coherently, and with sophistication**. Today's task builds that foundation.

- Choose one of the following topics and prepare for **3 minutes** (notes allowed — bullet points only, no full sentences):
  > *"The advantages and disadvantages of living in a large city"*
- Speak for **2 full minutes** without stopping. Record yourself.
- Evaluate using this checklist:
  - ☐ Did I use discourse markers to organise my ideas? (*"On the one hand...", "Furthermore...", "It's worth noting that..."*)
  - ☐ Did I give specific examples, not just general statements?
  - ☐ Did I vary my sentence length (mix of short punchy sentences and longer complex ones)?
  - ☐ Did I reach 2 minutes without running out of ideas?

---

#### 👂 Listening — *35 minutes*
**Activity: Listening for Attitude and Opinion**

At C1, you must identify not just *what* speakers say, but *how they feel* about it.

- Listen to a **BBC Radio 4 podcast or discussion programme** (e.g., *"The Moral Maze"* or *"Desert Island Discs"*) for 15 minutes.
- As you listen, note:
  - The speaker's **attitude** (enthusiastic? sceptical? resigned? passionate?)
  - **Specific language** that signals their attitude (e.g., *"I'm afraid that...", "What strikes me as remarkable is..."*)
- This skill is directly tested in C1 listening tasks.

**🔗 Resource:** [BBC Radio 4 Podcasts](https://www.bbc.co.uk/radio4/podcasts)

---

#### 🌉 Mediation — *30 minutes*
**Activity: Oral Mediation Simulation**

- Find a **short infographic or data chart** online (try BBC News or Statista — search for something like *"social media usage statistics"* or *"climate change data"*).
- **Explain the key information aloud** for 90 seconds as if you're presenting it to someone who cannot see the image.
- Focus on:
  - Describing trends (*"There has been a sharp increase in...", "The figures suggest that..."*)
  - Highlighting the most significant data points
  - Offering a brief personal evaluation (*"What I find most striking is..."*)

**🔗 Resource:** [Statista Free Charts](https://www.statista.com) or [BBC News Explainers](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/explainers)

#### ✍️ Writing — *50 minutes*
**Activity: Essay Structure Deconstruction**

Before writing great essays, you need to **understand what makes them great**.

- Find a **model C1 essay** from the Cambridge English website or search *"C1 Advanced model essay with examiner comments"*.
- Read it carefully and **annotate** it:
  - Underline the thesis statement
  - Label each paragraph's function (introduction / argument 1 / counter-argument / conclusion)
  - Circle all discourse markers and linking phrases
  - Star any vocabulary that feels genuinely C1-level
- Then, **rewrite the introduction** of the essay in your own words, keeping the same structure but using different vocabulary.

**🔗 Resource:** [Cambridge English Sample Writing with Examiner Comments](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/advanced/preparation/)

#### 📖 Reading — *40 minutes*
**Activity: Vocabulary in Context — Building Your C1 Word Bank**

- Read any article from **The Economist** (many articles are free) or **The Guardian Opinion section**.
- As you read, identify **10 words or phrases** that feel genuinely advanced — things you understand but wouldn't naturally produce yourself.
- For each one, record in your notebook:
  - The word/phrase
  - The sentence it appeared in
  - A synonym or definition
  - Your own example sentence
- This is the beginning of your **personal C1 vocabulary bank** — add to it every day this week.

**🔗 Resources:**

### 🗓️ Thursday — *"Listening Deeply, Writing Precisely"*
**Theme: Listening Detail & Writing Accuracy**

---

#### 🗣️ Speaking — *30 minutes*
**Activity: Responding to Abstract Questions**

C1 speaking often involves **abstract, philosophical, or hypothetical questions**. Today, practise responding to these without preparation time.

- Ask a friend, family member, or language partner to ask you these questions one by one (or read them yourself and respond immediately):
  1. *"Is it possible to be truly objective about anything?"*
  2. *"What does success mean to you, and has your definition changed?"*
  3. *"Should art always have a social purpose?"*
- Aim for **45–60 seconds per answer**. Focus on **not saying "I don't know"** — instead, use phrases like:
  - *"That's a thought-provoking question. I'd argue that..."*
  - *"It depends on how you define..., but generally speaking..."*

---

#### 👂 Listening — *45 minutes*
**Activity: Note-Taking Under Listening Conditions**

This directly mirrors the C1 exam listening format.

- Find a **lecture or talk on YouTube** (try **BBC Ideas**, **RSA Animate**, or a university open lecture) lasting 10–12 minutes.
- Listen **once only** and take notes using this structure:
  - Main topic:
  - Key point 1 + supporting detail:
  - Key point 2 + supporting detail:
  - Speaker's conclusion or recommendation:
- After listening, write a **150-word summary** using only your notes (not the video). This trains the exact skill needed for C1 listening-into-writing tasks.

**🔗 Resources:**
- [BBC Ideas](https://www.bbc.co.uk/ideas)
- [RSA Animate YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/user/theRSAorg)

#### 🌉 Mediation — *35 minutes*
**Activity: Cross-Language Concept Mediation**

Think of a concept, tradition, or idea from your own culture or first language that doesn't translate easily into English.

- Write a **120-word explanation** of this concept for an English-speaking audience who has no knowledge of your culture.
- Focus on:
  - Defining the concept clearly
  - Using analogies (*"It's similar to..., but with the key difference that..."*)
  - Conveying cultural nuance without oversimplifying
- This type of task builds the **cultural mediation** awareness that C1 examiners reward.

---

#### ✍️ Writing — *50 minutes*
**Activity: Paragraph-Level Writing Practice**

Today, focus on writing **one perfect paragraph** rather than a full essay.

- Write a **single body paragraph** (120–150 words) on this topic:
  > *"The internet has fundamentally changed the way we form relationships."*
- Your paragraph must include:
  - A clear **topic sentence**
  - **Two pieces of evidence or examples** (real or hypothetical)
  - A **linking sentence** that connects to a broader argument
  - At least **one complex grammatical structure** (e.g., a relative clause, passive construction, or conditional)
- Read it aloud when finished. Does it sound like C1? Does every sentence earn its place?

#### 📖 Reading — *40 minutes*
**Activity: Multiple Choice Reading Practice**

- Complete **Part 1 of a C1 Advanced Reading sample paper** (multiple choice — 4 options per question, based on a long text).
- This part tests your ability to understand **detail, opinion, attitude, tone, and implication** — not just surface meaning.
- After checking answers, for every wrong answer:
  - Find the exact line in the text that proves the correct answer
  - Identify **why the distractor you chose was wrong** (this is the most valuable learning step)

## 💡 Three C1-Specific Tips for Week 1

---

### Tip 1: 🎯 Stop Aiming for "Correct" — Aim for "Sophisticated"
At B2, the goal is accuracy. At C1, accuracy is assumed — the examiner is now looking for **range, nuance, and sophistication**. This week, every time you write or speak, ask yourself: *"Could I say this in a more precise, varied, or interesting way?"* Replace *"very important"* with *"paramount"* or *"of considerable significance."* Replace *"I think"* with *"I would argue"* or *"It strikes me that."* Small upgrades, compounded daily, create a C1 voice.

---

### Tip 2: 📓 Build Your Vocabulary Bank Like a Collector, Not a Student
Don't just write down words and their definitions — that's what you did at A2. At C1, collect **words in context**: the full sentence, the register (formal/informal), the collocations (*"reach a consensus," "draw a distinction," "pose a challenge"*), and your own example. Aim for **5–8 new items per day** this week. By Week 8, you'll have a personal lexical resource that no coursebook can replicate.

---

### Tip 3: 🎙️ Record Yourself — Even If You Hate It
The single most underused tool in language learning is the voice recorder on your phone. C1 speaking candidates who have **never heard themselves speak English** are always shocked by the gap between how they think they sound and how they actually sound. This week, record every speaking task. You don't need to share it with anyone. Just listen back once, note one thing to improve, and move on. By Week 7, you'll hear the difference — and it will be remarkable.
